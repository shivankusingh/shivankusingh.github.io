Project 01
2026 – now

Buster the Panda

A small red panda robot that walks, rears up into a tripod on its hind legs and tail, and comes back down. Every movement is learned with PPO in simulation, against the real servos' speed and torque limits, so it can transfer to hardware.

Training

Reverse curriculum

Rearing starts from the finished pose and steps back toward standing, one rung at a time, with replay of rungs it has already passed.

Robustness

Stress-tested

Pushes, mass ±10% and randomized physics: all 20 stress conditions pass, and a policy switcher chains the skills together.

Hardware

Sim before solder

Joint torques are logged in simulation before any parts are bought. Sim also ruled out jumping: about 2 mm of lift against the 6 cm it would need.

Project 02
2026 – now

Bug Bot

A real animal's brain on a chip. The C. elegans worm's connectome runs as an integer spiking neural network on a Basys 3 FPGA. Touch its head and the robot backs up. Touch its tail and it goes forward.

BASYS 3 · XC7A35TLD15 … LD0
wheels: idle

This panel uses the board's LED layout: LD15 backward, LD14 forward, LD13 step tick, LD6–LD0 wheel speed in binary. Its timing is a simplified sketch. The real readout comes from the 300-neuron network.

Live demo

A 3D view of all 300 neurons next to a simulated Basys 3 replaying the chip design.

Open in new tab ↗

Neurons

Integer LIF

Each step the voltage leaks by v >> 3 and adds its inputs. A neuron fires at 256 and resets. Every value fits in 16 bits.

Hardware

Time-multiplexed core

One neuron circuit is reused for every neuron, with the wiring stored in memory. The same Verilog runs 4 neurons or the whole worm.

Verification

Bit-exact

A Python reference model is checked against the Verilog with cocotb, every voltage and every spike, before anything goes on the chip.
